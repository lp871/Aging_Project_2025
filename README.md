# Cellular and spatial architecture of retinal aging across vertebrate species.

## Abstract

Aging is the major risk factor for retinal degeneration, but the cellular programs governing retinal aging remain poorly understood. Here, we generated a cross-species atlas of retinal and retinal pigment epithelium aging by integrating single-nucleus transcriptomic and chromatin accessibility profiling with spatial transcriptomics across zebrafish, mice, and humans. We identified extensive cell type- and species-specific aging trajectories, together with conserved programs involving inflammation, metabolic remodeling, loss of specialized retinal functions, and altered cellular homeostasis. Cell type-specific aging clocks accurately predicted biological age and revealed dynamic, reversible shifts in cellular age following acute injury. Notably, human retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells strongly upregulated aging hallmark-associated genes, which was further enhanced in disease models, and partially reversed by metformin treatment. Spatial transcriptomic analysis revealed region-specific aging signatures and proximity effects, with Müller glia exhibiting pro-rejuvenative influences on neighboring neurons. Targeted Müller glia-specific induction of Yamanaka factors reduced molecular age in rod photoreceptors and bipolar cells. Our study defines the cellular architecture of retinal aging and identifies potential targets for rejuvenation.
